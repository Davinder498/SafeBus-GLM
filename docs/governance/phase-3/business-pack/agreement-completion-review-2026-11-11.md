## Proposed core wording for integration after review

These proposals are not an additional incorporated schedule and do not amend the existing draft until reconciled with the decisions below.

**DPA §1:** Personal information has the meaning in POPA. Customer service records remain under the Customer's control and subject to POPA and ATIA. SafeBus complies in its statutory service-provider capacity; this creates no employment relationship for labour purposes.

**DPA §4:** Only expressly authorized subcontractors may handle Customer information. SafeBus must bind them to corresponding privacy obligations and remains responsible for their performance. Silence is not consent.

**DPA §6:** A breach includes loss, unauthorized access or unauthorized disclosure of personal information. SafeBus must immediately notify the Customer's privacy contact on learning of a breach, supply available facts, contain the incident and provide further updates. An incomplete investigation does not postpone initial notice.

**MSA §9:** Confidentiality survives termination indefinitely.

## Decisions needing the owner, with proposed operating mechanics

### B1 — Supplier authorization

Recommendation: the administrator's initial explicit acceptance includes the exact, complete supplier appendix. Later material additions or country changes require a new affirmative recorded approval before the affected authority's information uses that change. Offer 30 calendar days' advance notice for planned changes. Existing approved processing can continue while awaiting a decision. If a change is unavoidable and not accepted, explain the specific service impact and agree an alternative or exit; never silently switch providers or treat inactivity as agreement.

Urgent containment may stop or isolate affected processing immediately. It must not become blanket permission to introduce an unapproved supplier. No global “all customers accepted” flag; approval belongs to each authority. Implementation needs version-specific acceptance and a server-side check at the affected processing path, not just an email notice. Verify actual vendor flow-down terms before offering this clause.

Owner choice: approve this affirmative-approval model, or request an alternative for legal review. Previous 30-day notice-only recommendation is superseded as a proposal, not recorded as approved.

### B2 — Incident staffing and request handling

Recommendation: immediate initial incident notice upon awareness; the earlier 24-hour proposal must not be represented as permission to delay. The owner currently has no independent responder. A functioning escalation route and an absence arrangement must be demonstrated before promising this service. Automated monitoring does not establish continuous human coverage.

For ordinary authority requests, propose acknowledgment within two business days and an action plan within five business days, both from receipt during the existing Alberta support calendar. Earlier statutory/customer deadlines take precedence and need escalation. This is not a five-day completion guarantee. Requests from individuals are routed to the authority; SafeBus does not decide the authority's disclosure rights or silently become its access office.

Owner input needed: confirm willingness to undertake immediate incident escalation and identify an absence/backup arrangement (identity privately), and approve or amend the ordinary request targets. Do not invent a backup contact or purchase outside coverage.

### B3 — Assisted exit

Keep the approved district self-service model and no automatic 30-day export/60-day purge promise. Add a tested assisted process for records beyond directory CSVs. Recommendation: ordinary request targets above, followed by an explicit scoped completion date accepted with that authority, before activation of a customer contract that relies on it. The accepted order must specify return format, secure delivery, remaining copies, disposal dates/triggers and holds. Leaving these undecided until an authority wants to leave is not completion.

Proposed deliverable: a tenant-specific export with a manifest of included/omitted categories and machine-readable tables, transfer confirmation, then a deletion report distinguishing verified actions, permitted retained exceptions and provider expiry. Do not promise a complete export, selective backup erasure or a deletion certificate until corresponding operations are tested.

Owner choice: approve including scoped assisted exit within the standard subscription, or identify a separate fee policy for review. No fee is introduced by this review. Engineering owns discovery of actual data categories and provider retention; the owner should not guess technical windows.
